How grading works

Every call and every signal is graded from public price. Nothing is self-reported. Losses stay up.

A trader’s call

  • A call is an entry, a stop and a target, published to the feed. Once published it is permanent: the levels can’t be edited and the call can’t be deleted.
  • It is graded on public Orderly candles. Calls published from November 13, 2026 are graded on 1-minute candles. Earlier calls, on 1-hour candles.
  • The entry must trade. An entry within 0.3% of the market price at publish fills at once. Any other entry fills only when price reaches it. A call that never fills is never graded.
  • First touch decides. Target first = a win, paid in R from the posted levels. Stop first = −1R. Both in the same candle = a loss.
  • A stop or target on the wrong side of the entry = not graded.
  • The caller board lists only callers with 5+ graded calls and a net-positive record in R.

Our own signals

  • Each signal is registered with its rule and a date before it is judged. Only events after that date count. A changed rule is a new signal, registered again.
  • Each event is graded in R on one fixed contract: stop at 1.2× the coin’s 4-hour ATR, target at 1.5× the stop, closed after 168 hours if neither is hit.
  • Every signal is tested against random entries on the same market, side and window, and on coins it was never picked on.

The stages, in order:

  • Testing: under 30 new events, or no positive mean R yet.
  • Lead: 30+ new events, mean R above 0, both halves agree.
  • Edge: beats 95% of random entries and repeats on the holdout coins.
  • Tradeable: its exit nets above 0 after fees over 30+ new trades.
  • Paper: a paper agent trades it. 30 paper trades, judged after fees, before any live plan.
  • Retired: 100+ events with mean R at or below 0, or worse than random. It stays on the record.
